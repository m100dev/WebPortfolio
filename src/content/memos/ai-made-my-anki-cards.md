Every flashcard in my CCNA deck was typed by hand. Subnetting, OSPF, port numbers, all of it. It worked, but it took a long time, and a lot of the time I set aside for studying went into making cards instead of reviewing them.

These days I use AI as my tutor, and that changed how I build my decks.

When I start a new topic, or one I'm having a hard time grasping, I go over it with AI first. I ask detailed questions and work through how the pieces connect until I'm confident I actually understand it, not just that I've read it. Once the session is done, I have it create detailed flashcards based on what we covered and the topics I struggled with.

I still review every card before it goes into my deck. If something's wrong, unclear, or not how I'd want to be tested on it, I fix it or drop it. That way I'm still part of the verification, and nothing ends up in the deck that I haven't read and agreed with.

The part that matters most to me is the review. Sure, I could keep typing every card by hand, but if that leaves me no time to actually review them, what's the point? Spaced repetition only works if you show up for the reviews. Letting AI handle the card writing means my time goes where it counts.

This is exactly how I built the Anki deck for my Azure AVD Enterprise lab. It's a big lab with a lot of moving parts, and making those cards by hand would have taken me days.

Using AI for efficiency doesn't always mean finding something new for it to do. Sometimes it's just looking at a habit you already have and seeing how AI can speed it up. For me, that was flashcards.
